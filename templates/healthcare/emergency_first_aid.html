Emergency Preparedness & First Aid

Emergency preparedness means knowing how to respond safely when an accident, injury or sudden health emergency occurs. Basic first-aid awareness can help people provide immediate support while professional medical assistance is arranged.

🚨 Emergency Situation Quick Guide

1. What Is First Aid?

First aid is the immediate basic care given to a person who is injured or suddenly becomes unwell until appropriate professional medical care is available.

  • Stay calm and assess the situation.
  • Make sure the area is safe.
  • Call for appropriate emergency assistance.
  • Provide basic help within your level of training.

2. Emergency Preparedness at Home

Every household should have a simple plan for dealing with emergencies.

  • Keep important emergency contact numbers available.
  • Know the location of the nearest healthcare facility.
  • Keep a basic first-aid kit accessible.
  • Store important medical information safely.
  • Teach family members basic emergency awareness.

3. Basic First-Aid Kit

A basic first-aid kit can contain simple supplies for minor injuries. It should be stored in a clean, dry and easily accessible place.

  • Clean bandages and adhesive strips
  • Sterile gauze
  • Clean gloves
  • Antiseptic supplies suitable for first aid
  • Clean scissors
  • Emergency contact information
Important: Check the first-aid kit regularly and replace damaged or expired supplies.

4. Cuts & Minor Bleeding

For a minor cut, basic first aid includes protecting yourself from contact with blood, cleaning the injury appropriately and covering it with a clean dressing.

  • Use clean hands or gloves when providing help.
  • Apply appropriate direct pressure for bleeding.
  • Cover the injury with a clean dressing.
  • Seek medical attention if bleeding is severe or does not stop.

⚠ Severe Bleeding

Severe bleeding is an emergency. Contact emergency medical services immediately and provide appropriate first aid while waiting for professional help.

5. Burns

Burns should be taken seriously. For a minor thermal burn, cooling the affected area with cool running water can help. Avoid applying household substances to a burn.

  • Move away from the source of heat if it is safe to do so.
  • Cool the burn with cool running water.
  • Do not apply toothpaste, oil or other household substances.
  • Do not deliberately break blisters.
  • Seek medical help for serious or extensive burns.

6. Fractures & Injuries

If a fracture or serious injury is suspected, avoid unnecessary movement of the injured area and seek professional medical assistance.

  • Keep the injured person as comfortable as possible.
  • Avoid unnecessary movement.
  • Do not attempt to straighten a suspected broken bone.
  • Seek medical attention promptly.

7. Fainting & Unconsciousness

A person who becomes unconscious requires careful attention. Check whether they respond and seek emergency assistance when necessary.

  • Make sure the surrounding area is safe.
  • Check for responsiveness.
  • Call for emergency medical assistance if the person is unresponsive.
  • Follow instructions provided by emergency professionals.
  • Do not give food or drink to an unconscious person.

8. Poisoning & Chemical Exposure

Suspected poisoning or exposure to harmful chemicals can be a medical emergency.

  • Move away from the source if it is safe.
  • Avoid direct contact with the substance.
  • Seek emergency medical advice immediately.
  • Keep the product container or label available if it is safe to do so.
  • Do not make a person vomit unless specifically instructed by a qualified professional.

9. Emergency Contact & Communication

During an emergency, clear communication can help emergency responders reach the person more effectively.

  • In India, the pan-India emergency number is 112.
  • Clearly provide the location of the emergency.
  • Explain what happened and the person's condition.
  • Follow instructions from emergency responders.
  • Keep important medical information available when possible.

🚨 Emergency

For a life-threatening emergency, contact emergency services immediately rather than relying only on online health information.

10. Myths & Facts

Myth: Toothpaste should always be applied to burns.

Fact: Household substances such as toothpaste should not be used as burn treatment.
Myth: A suspected broken bone should always be straightened immediately.

Fact: Avoid trying to straighten a suspected fracture and seek professional medical help.
Myth: An unconscious person should be given water.

Fact: Do not give food or drink to an unconscious person.
