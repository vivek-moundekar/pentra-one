Mental Health Across All Ages

Mental health is an important part of overall health. People of all ages can experience stress, emotional difficulties or mental health concerns. Awareness, family support and timely professional help can support better well-being.

1. What Is Mental Health?

Mental health relates to how a person thinks, feels and behaves and how they manage everyday life, relationships and challenges.

  • Emotional well-being
  • Ability to handle everyday challenges
  • Healthy relationships
  • Ability to learn, work and participate in daily activities

2. Mental Health in Children

Children can experience emotional difficulties due to changes at home, school-related challenges, social situations or other experiences.

  • Listen to children without immediately judging them.
  • Provide a safe and supportive environment.
  • Encourage healthy sleep, food and physical activity.
  • Talk to a qualified professional when concerns continue.

3. Mental Health in Adolescents

Adolescence involves physical, emotional and social changes. Young people may experience academic pressure, relationship concerns, bullying or other sources of stress.

  • Encourage open communication.
  • Provide emotional support.
  • Encourage healthy daily routines.
  • Take concerns about persistent emotional changes seriously.
  • Seek professional support when needed.

4. Mental Health in Adults

Adults may experience stress related to work, education, finances, family responsibilities or other life situations.

  • Maintain social connections.
  • Stay physically active.
  • Maintain adequate sleep and rest.
  • Make time for relaxation.
  • Talk to trusted people about concerns.

5. Mental Health During Pregnancy & After Childbirth

Pregnancy and the period after childbirth can involve major physical and emotional changes. Mothers may need additional emotional and practical support during this period.

  • Encourage family and social support.
  • Talk openly about emotional concerns.
  • Do not ignore persistent sadness or anxiety.
  • Contact a healthcare professional when concerns are significant.
Remember: Experiencing emotional difficulties does not mean someone is weak. Mental health concerns deserve care and support.

6. Mental Health in Older Adults

Older adults may experience loneliness, grief, changes in health or changes in daily life. Social connection and appropriate healthcare support can be important.

  • Stay connected with family and community.
  • Encourage suitable physical activity.
  • Maintain regular daily routines.
  • Discuss persistent emotional concerns with a professional.

7. Managing Everyday Stress

Some stress is a normal part of life. Healthy coping strategies can help people manage everyday challenges.

  • Talk to trusted friends or family members.
  • Take breaks and make time for relaxation.
  • Maintain regular physical activity.
  • Maintain healthy sleep habits.
  • Organize daily tasks and responsibilities.

8. Reducing Mental Health Stigma

Stigma can prevent people from talking about mental health or seeking support. Creating a respectful environment can make it easier for people to ask for help.

  • Listen without judgment.
  • Avoid making fun of mental health concerns.
  • Respect people's privacy.
  • Encourage appropriate professional support.
  • Share reliable health information.

9. When to Seek Professional Help

If emotional or mental health difficulties continue, become severe or interfere significantly with daily life, professional support may be appropriate.

⚠ Important

If someone is in immediate danger, has thoughts of harming themselves or others, or is experiencing a serious mental health emergency, seek urgent professional or emergency assistance. Do not leave the person alone if immediate safety is a concern.

10. Myths & Facts

Myth: Mental health problems only happen to adults.

Fact: Mental health concerns can affect people at different stages of life, including children and older adults.
Myth: Talking about mental health makes a person weak.

Fact: Talking to a trusted person or professional can be an important step toward getting support.
Myth: People should simply ignore mental health problems.

Fact: Persistent or serious concerns deserve appropriate attention and professional support.
